# Build the PlanktonOTC backend against verified rails

The Robinhood Chain rails PlanktonOTC needs are mostly real and machine-readable today, but the price inputs for its niche are the weak point. Chain 4663 is an Arbitrum-stack L2 with sub-second soft finality, USDG is a live 6-decimal EIP-2612/EIP-3009 token, and Robinhood publishes free REST endpoints for **203 active stock tokens**, including roughly 20 plausible sub-$2B names. No Chainlink feed address, heartbeat or small-cap coverage list could be extracted, and Pyth's extended-hours equity coverage is only about 50 large caps behind a $5,000 per month plan. No venue in the research lists US stocks under $2B, so PlanktonOTC has no reference design for its core product. The two defining 2026 incidents, Ostium's roughly $23.75M oracle-signer exploit and trade.xyz's SK Hynix single-print liquidation cascade, both failed in the layers PlanktonOTC must build itself: signer custody, price bounds, session handling and liquidation design. The research did not include the spec text itself. The "spec claims" table below therefore covers only claims that the research briefs echoed, so the team must re-run it against the full spec.

## The chain, USDG and stock-token rails are verifiable and mostly ready

Mainnet is chain ID **4663** (confirmed on-chain as 0x1237), testnet is 46630, gas is ETH, and the stack is Arbitrum Dedicated Blockchains with first-come-first-served sequencing ([Robinhood Chain docs](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain), [Connecting](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/connecting)). The public RPC is rate-limited and "not recommended for production". Docs recommend Alchemy, and Alchemy offers no Debug or Trace API for this chain ([Alchemy](https://www.alchemy.com/rpc/robinhood.md)). The `wss://feed...` endpoints are raw Arbitrum sequencer feeds, not `eth_subscribe` sockets, so event streaming needs a provider WebSocket ([Connecting](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/connecting)). A direct probe measured blocks at roughly **0.11 s average**, with a "finalized" tag about 7,600 blocks behind head. Soft finality is the sequencer receipt, and Ethereum finality arrives about 13 minutes after batch posting ([Transaction finality](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/transaction-finality)). Confirmation depth must therefore be defined in time or by the finalized tag, not a block count. FCFS ordering with a zero priority fee means keepers compete on latency, not gas bids, and a single Robinhood-operated sequencer is the only liveness path, with the L1 delayed inbox as force-inclusion fallback ([Protocol contracts](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/protocol-contracts)). Canonical Multicall3 (0xcA11...CA11) has bytecode on mainnet, and Permit2 and WETH addresses are published ([Protocol contracts](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/protocol-contracts)).

USDG at `0x5fc5360D0400a0Fd4f2af552ADD042D716F1d168` is confirmed by Robinhood docs ([Contracts](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/contracts)). The research's own on-chain probes show an upgradeable UUPS proxy with 6 decimals, name "Global Dollar", and a live `paused()` (currently false). It has an OpenZeppelin AccessControl pauser role, an owner, and a `supplyControl` address. It exposes EIP-2612 and EIP-3009 typehashes. The reported `DOMAIN_SEPARATOR` was recomputed and matched with name "Global Dollar", version "1", chain 4663 and the proxy as verifying contract. `version()` and `eip712Domain()` both revert, so the domain must be hardcoded and tested. Which signature encodings are accepted (v,r,s only versus ERC-1271 bytes) and the freeze or blacklist function names remain unread because Blockscout returned 403, so the team must read the verified source manually. The design assumption must be that Paxos can pause, upgrade or freeze balances, which is an operational and solvency risk for a USDG-settled pool.

Stock tokens are 18-decimal ERC-20s implementing ERC-8056 (Scaled UI Amount), where raw balances stay fixed and `uiMultiplier()` (1e18 scale) carries dividends and splits ([Building with Stock Tokens](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/building-with-stock-tokens)). Canonical identity is by address only, and the docs state that a matching name or ticker at a different address is not a Robinhood Stock Token ([Token Contracts](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/contracts)). The live `GET https://api.robinhood.com/rhj/assets` returned 203 active entries on chain 4663, `/rhj/prices` returned 203 quotes, and `/rhj/corporate-actions` returned 34 cash-dividend rows with no splits ([Stock Token APIs](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/stock-token-apis), live observation 2026-10-30). The live payloads carry undocumented fields (`tokenBid`/`tokenAsk` multiplier-adjusted, `dailyHigh`, `dailyLow`, `isin`, `tokenDecimals`) and a `tradingCapabilities` shape that contradicts the docs page. The documented limit is **60 req/s per endpoint**, with 15 s price caching and 1 h corporate-action caching, and one bulk `/prices` call covers all tokens. The Chain Terms of Service reviewed by keyword contain no clause on redistribution or commercial reuse of this data, so legal sign-off is still required. The mint/burn window is Monday 02:00 to Saturday 02:00 CET/CEST, so it shifts in UTC across DST ([Stock Tokens overview](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/stock-tokens)).

## Price inputs are the weak link, and small caps fall in the gap

Chainlink publishes one feed per stock token that returns "Total Return Value", the underlying price already multiplied by `uiMultiplier`, so the multiplier must not be applied again. The feeds follow Chainlink's 24/5 equity sessions (regular, pre, post, overnight) and carry **no heartbeat off-hours**, so `updatedAt` staleness is the integrator's own check ([Chainlink docs](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/tokenized-equity-feeds/robinhood.md), [Robinhood oracles](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/oracles-and-price-feeds)). Robinhood sets `oraclePaused()` around corporate actions, but the flag is advisory and unenforced on-chain ([Robinhood oracles](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/oracles-and-price-feeds)). Robinhood can also stage a multiplier change for a future timestamp, and `newUIMultiplier()` and `effectiveAt()` return non-zero values even when nothing is pending, so `effectiveAt` must be compared against block time (live probe on AAPL). The Chainlink feed table was empty in the fetch, so **no feed address, decimals, heartbeat or deviation is verified**, and the team should copy the proxy table from Chainlink's page in a real browser. Robinhood's docs explicitly say not to hardcode proxies. Chainlink's feed coverage appears liquidity-gated ("where underlying liquidity and session data quality support it"), and token existence does not imply a feed, which is the main threat to a small-cap-only product. Robinhood recommends the L2 Sequencer Uptime Feed, yet Chainlink's list omits Robinhood Chain and says it is no longer expanding that product ([Chainlink L2 feeds](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/l2-sequencer-feeds.md)), so no sequencer feed can be assumed.

Pyth does exist on the chain: its EVM contract page lists Robinhood Chain with Pyth Pro and Core addresses (taken from a fetch summary, to be re-checked) ([Pyth docs](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/contract-addresses/evm)). Extended-hours feeds (.PRE, .POST, .ON) moved from Core to Pro, which needs an API key, costs **$5,000 per month** for US equities, and covers about 50 tickers such as AAPL, TSLA, NVDA, COIN, MSTR and SPY ([Pyth blog](https://www.pyth.network/blog/extended-hours-us-equity-data-moves-to-pyth-pro)). That is a large-cap set, so for a sub-$2B universe neither Pyth nor Chainlink is demonstrated to cover the names. The free Robinhood `/rhj/prices` bid/ask for all 203 tokens, with `dailyTradingVolume`, `isTradingHalt` and `generatedAt`, is the only source verified to cover the small-cap candidates. It is a single-vendor, off-chain, 15 s-cached source with no stated SLA. It must be treated as a reference input that is cross-checked, not a sole truth. The research lists candidate sub-$2B symbols such as AEHR, ABCL, AXTI, BB, CLOV, IBRX, INOD, MXL, NNE, NVTS, POET, QUBT, RCAT, RDW, SLS, SOUN, USAR, VICR, WYFI and XNDU, but these come from the researcher's general knowledge and not from market-cap data ([Stock Tokens research, live registry](https://api.robinhood.com/rhj/assets)).

## Two 2026 incidents define what the mark engine and signer must prevent

On July 15, 2026 an attacker holding an authorised oracle-signer key plus a registered PriceUpKeep forwarder submitted a future-dated, validly signed BTC-USD report to Ostium and cycled positions against the OLP vault for about **23.75M USDC** ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/410122/ostium-post-mortem-exploit), [Galaxy](https://galaxy.com/insights/research/ostium-left-an-opening-for-exploiters-and-24m-went-out-the-door)). Galaxy's reading is that the verifier "simply validates the signer's identity" and not price accuracy. A 100 USDC test position produced about 897.8 USDC of fake profit, and the main atomic batch was about 11.9M USDC. Ostium attributes the breach to off-chain infrastructure, not contract logic. The figure is single-lineage (Arkham via Galaxy plus Ostium's own account). What the research did not establish is whether Ostium had any band or rate limit, so the lessons below are inferences.

On July 28, 2026 KST a single-share print at the daily lower limit (KRW 1,272,000 against a 1,816,000 close, about -30%) on Nextrade's 08:00 pre-market became trade.xyz's SK Hynix oracle input. KRX did not open until 09:00, and the oracle had no outlier rejection, size weighting, cross-venue check or prior-close bound. The oracle fell about 15.6% (band-limited), roughly 960 long accounts were liquidated, and about 100 profitable shorts were auto-deleveraged ([Nexus Mutual](https://nexusmutual.io/blog/xyz-skhynix-flash-crash-on-hyperliquid-incident-report), [Galaxy](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/hyperliquid-tradexyz-oracle-liquidations)). Reported size varies: $57.4M position value and $17.3M realised margin loss (Nexus) against $57M to $80M and a -18.7% mark move (Galaxy), because the figures measure oracle versus mark. The reimbursement reported was about $1.45M ([Edgex](https://pro.edgex.exchange/en-US/news/article/trade-xyz-skhynix-compensation)). Galaxy's conclusion matters for design: more oracles would not have helped because all read the same book, and "correct price discovery is not the same thing as sound liquidation design". trade.xyz's mark is the median of oracle, oracle plus a 150 s EMA of book deviation, and the median of best bid, ask and last trade ([Galaxy](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/hyperliquid-tradexyz-oracle-liquidations)). Small caps behave like that Korean pre-market: thin, gappy and prone to odd-lot prints, which makes this incident the closest analogue to PlanktonOTC's whole universe.

The reference designs offer reusable mechanics but none for small caps. Synthetix SIP-279 fills at the average of a linear premium (`premium = skew / skewScale`) before and after the trade, which is path-independent and closed-form for a signed quote ([SIP-279](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Synthetixio/SIPs/master/content/sips/sip-279.md)). GMX v2 signs both min and max prices so the contract can pick the side adverse to the trader. It caps pending trader PnL with a max-PnL factor and tells oracle keepers to stop signing if the sequencer halts ([gmx-synthetics](https://github.com/gmx-io/gmx-synthetics/blob/main/README.md)). Avantis's dual-oracle reject-on-divergence and junior/senior tranche model were seen only in secondary summaries, and the Zellic tranche-waterfall finding mentioned in the brief was **not found**. No open-source TypeScript keeper, quote signer or EIP-712 nonce-pattern reference was identified, so the team should not assume any exists.

## Calendars and halts are cheap to build but not fully verified

NYSE publishes the full 2026 and 2027 holiday sets with 13:00 ET early closes (2026: Nov 26 closed, Nov 27 and Dec 24 early, Dec 25 closed; 2027: eleven closures plus a Nov 26 early close), and Nasdaq agrees for 2026 ([NYSE](https://nyse.com/trade/hours-calendars), [Nasdaq Trader](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/Trader.aspx?id=Calendar)). Nasdaq's 2027 list was not verified, and no official machine-readable calendar or licence terms were found, so a hand-maintained JSON file of about 11 dates per year with a Nasdaq cross-check is the pragmatic path. Halts come from `https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/rss.aspx?feed=tradehalts`, an RSS 2.0 feed in an `ndaq:` namespace with a 1-minute TTL and fields for symbol, halt date and time, reason code, resumption quote and trade times ([Nasdaq halt feed](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/rss.aspx?feed=tradehalts)). The live sample showed an LUDP pause with a 5-minute halt-to-trade-resumption gap. The code table separates short volatility pauses (LUDP, LUDS, M, T5, T7) from open-ended regulatory halts (T1, T2, T12, H4, H9, H10, H11, O1) ([Nasdaq halt codes](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/Trader.aspx?id=TradeHaltCodes)). Whether NYSE and Arca-listed halts appear in this feed, its licence terms and its latency against the true halt are all unverified. Small caps are disproportionately exposed to LULD pauses and regulatory halts, so this gap affects the core product. Robinhood's `isTradingHalt` flag was false for every token during the observed session, and its behaviour on weekends or during a real halt is unobserved.

## Regulatory context points to a strict geofence

The research found no US authorisation for equity perps. The CFTC's May 29, 2026 package approved a cash-settled bitcoin perpetual on a DCM. Its staff interpretation on offshore access does not extend to perps on other asset classes, and a platform serving US persons with swap-classified perps may need SEF registration ([Cahill](https://www.cahill.com/publications/client-alerts/2026-06-02-true-perpetuals-come-onshore), [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/cftc-advances-framework-for-perpetual-contracts-and-247-markets)). The SEC's January 28, 2026 staff statement treats third-party tokenized securities as custodial or synthetic, and synthetics are reportedly excluded from the innovation exemption ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/statement-on-tokenized-securities-4199739/), [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/newsletters/layer-one/2026-05-21-layer-one-tokenized-stock-innovation-exemptions-410800)). The FCA named Hyperliquid on May 21, 2026 for failing to block UK users ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/370117/hyperliquid-hit-by-uk-fca-warning-as-crypto-perps-face-scrutiny)), and ESMA said in February 2026 that many crypto perps likely qualify as CFDs ([Ledger Insights](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/eu-regulator-esma-warns-on-crypto-perpetuals-but-also-applies-to-tokenized-stocks/); detail unverified). Robinhood's stock tokens are themselves restricted in the US, Canada, UK and Switzerland ([Stock Tokens overview](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/stock-tokens)), and Lighter's Robinhood Wallet instance excludes the US, UK, Canada, Switzerland, UAE and Singapore ([The Block](https://theblock.co/post/406918/robinhood-chain-goes-live-mainnet-alongside-24-7-tokenized-stocks-lighter-perps-planned-cryp)). A working geofence set is therefore the US and territories, UK, Canada, Switzerland, EU retail and sanctioned regions, with UAE unresearched. This is engineering awareness, not legal advice. No sourced guidance on geofencing practice (IP blocking, attestation, wallet screening) or on 2026 enforcement against a perp DEX was found.

## Concrete design implications

1. **Data ingest.** Poll `/rhj/prices` once per 15 s for all tokens and `/rhj/corporate-actions` hourly, validate with a tolerant schema (decimals as strings, unknown fields passed through), and sort corporate actions client-side because ordering is not reliable. Treat `generatedAt` age and `dailyTradingVolume` as quality inputs, and keep a second independent source behind it.
2. **Mark engine.** Never let a single print move the mark. Bound every input against the prior close and the Chainlink value, require minimum size or volume, and add a hold or liquidation grace after any reopen from a closed session. Use a trade.xyz-style median of oracle, smoothed book deviation and book mid, and make liquidation use the smoothed, band-bounded price. Use Chainlink only after checking staleness, `oraclePaused()` and `effectiveAt`.
3. **Chain bindings.** Define the viem chain with id 4663, resolve token and feed addresses from config generated at build time, and verify canonical status by address against the registry. Hardcode the USDG EIP-712 domain, add a test comparing it to `DOMAIN_SEPARATOR()`, and measure confirmations in seconds or finalized tag, not block counts. Use a paid provider with a provider WebSocket, with the public RPC only as a fallback.
4. **Calendar.** Keep a versioned NYSE and Nasdaq JSON calendar with early closes at 13:00 ET and extended-hours sessions, driven by IANA time zones (`America/New_York`, and `Europe/Berlin` for the mint/burn window), and alert when the next year's list is missing.
5. **Halts.** Poll the Nasdaq halt feed every 30 to 60 s, dedupe on symbol, date and time, and treat times as nullable Eastern. Map short pauses and open-ended halts to different states, with a close-only or no-new-risk mode during any halt and an anchored reopen after resumption. Cross-check against `isTradingHalt` and the corporate-action calendar.
6. **Signer.** Verify on-chain that each signed price has a timestamp not in the future, inside a staleness window, and within a deviation band against a trusted reference, because a valid signature alone is not enough. Use separate keys for the price signer, the keeper and the admin, held in KMS or HSM with threshold signing where possible. Sign min and max prices, and bind quotes to a nonce, expiry and chain ID.
7. **Keeper.** Expect latency-based competition under FCFS, run keepers near the sequencer endpoint, and make them stop on sequencer health failure. Add per-market caps from a loss budget, a max unrealised-PnL factor against pool value, per-block and per-epoch net PnL extraction limits, and a rule against opening and closing the same position at the same price timestamp.
8. **Compliance.** Build the geofence and wallet screening as a first-class service, with a USDG pause or freeze runbook and a pre-agreed compensation or backstop policy.

## Conclusion

The research changes the build plan in one respect: the infrastructure risk is low, while the information risk is high. Chain, token, registry and calendar facts can be pinned down in days. The decisive unknown is whether any trustworthy, affordable price source exists for the thinnest names. No competitor has shown that market works, and the incidents that exist show that the failure sits in oracle bounds, session handling and liquidation design rather than in the contracts.

The practical implication is a staged launch. Start with the subset of sub-$2B tokens that has verified Chainlink coverage and reliable `/rhj` quotes. Make the signer's verification rules and halt and calendar gating the first components, and treat every unverified spec claim in the table above as a blocker for a public listing and not a documentation chore.
